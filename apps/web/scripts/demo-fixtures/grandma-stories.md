# A few stories from Iris

## On the lake house

Your grandfather and I bought that little lake house the summer the kids were small. The dock leaned, the kitchen radio only got two stations, and there was a screen door that slammed louder than a thunderclap. I loved every nail of it. Some afternoons I'd sit out with my tea and watch the wind on the water and think, this is what enough looks like.

## Sunday baking

Sunday was for cinnamon rolls. I learned the dough from my mother and she from hers, so when you smell the cinnamon you're smelling four women at once. Don't skip the second rise, sweetheart — I know you're impatient, you always were. Patience is just love spread thin.

## On grief

People will tell you grief gets smaller. It doesn't. You get bigger around it. You go to the lake, you put the kettle on, you let the wind do half the breathing for you, and one morning you wake up and find you've been carrying it without noticing.

## What to do when you don't know what to do

Now you listen. When you don't know what to do, do the next small kind thing. Bring someone tea. Send the letter. Sit in the garden for ten minutes. The world hardly ever needs your big heroic gesture; it almost always needs your next small kind thing.
